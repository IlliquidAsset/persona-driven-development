# System Persona-Driven Development (SPDD) - Twitter Thread

1/
Your AI writes code like a junior analyst builds a pro forma: it assumes everything goes according to plan. But markets are stochastic and systems are chaotic. If you aren't using System Persona-Driven Development (SPDD), you're building on a house of cards. 🧵

2/
LLMs are trained on idealized documentation. They write "Happy Path" code. In finance, that’s like a model with no downside scenarios. In dev, it’s "Behavioral Debt"—code that works in a vacuum but collapses under the first sign of system friction.

3/
Most devs treat APIs as deterministic machines. Input A -> Output B. But legacy systems have trauma. They lie. They have triggers. Treating a 20-year-old ERP like a clean REST API is a failure of due diligence. 

4/
When an LLM reads a Swagger doc, it sees a promise. When a Senior Dev looks at the same system, they see a minefield. We’ve been trying to prompt the AI to be "smarter," but we should be prompting it to be more "cynical."

5/
Enter System Legends. Instead of giving the AI a technical spec, I give it a CIA-level deep persona of the system itself. We aren't just documenting endpoints; we're mapping the "character" of the machine. #AI #SoftwareEngineering

6/
A System Legend covers 5 dimensions:
1. Habits (Actual behavior)
2. Lies (Where docs are wrong)
3. Triggers (What breaks it)
4. Trauma (Legacy constraints)
5. Incentives (What it "wants" to do)

7/
Don't tell the AI "The API returns a 404." Tell it "This system is an overworked clerk who says 'I don't know' (404) when they're actually just too busy to look (Timeout)." The AI's Theory of Mind kicks in.

8/
We’re moving from "integration" to "negotiation." The AI becomes a diplomat between systems. It stops blindly pushing data and starts navigating the friction, just like a VC navigating a messy cap table.

9/
I ran an experiment on a Legacy Ledger.
Standard Docs -> 15% data corruption under load.
System Legend -> 0% corruption.
The AI didn't get "smarter" at coding; it got better at anticipating the system's "mood swings." #LLM

10/
LLMs have a massive, untapped capacity for Theory of Mind. By personifying the system, we leverage the AI’s ability to reason about intent and behavior rather than just syntax. This is the core of SPDD.

11/
This is how you get "Simulated Seniority." A junior dev follows the manual. A senior dev knows where the bodies are buried. System Legends give the AI the "institutional memory" it lacks by default.

12/
In finance, we price in tail risk. In SPDD, we prompt for it. We are essentially building a "Stochastic Prompting" framework that respects the inherent chaos of interconnected software.

13/
This is the natural evolution of Persona-Driven Development (PDD). We started by giving the AI a persona. Now, we give the *target system* a persona. We’re creating a theater of operations where the AI knows every actor’s secrets.

14/
The future of dev isn't just writing better code; it's better system-to-system diplomacy. If your AI doesn't understand the "personality" of your stack, you're just automating the creation of technical debt. #PromptEngineering

15/
Imagine a CI/CD pipeline that auto-generates System Legends based on error logs and edge cases. A living, breathing "Legend" that evolves as the system decays or upgrades. That’s where we’re heading.

16/
Stop treating your APIs like math problems. Start treating them like counter-parties in a high-stakes negotiation. The results speak for themselves: 15% failure to 0%.
